Reviewed evidence summary, not the complete publication. Source: Space radiation measurements during the Artemis I lunar mission. Authors/institution: George SP et al.. Year: 2024. Evidence and environment: Physical dosimetry; uncrewed Artemis I Orion; detectors and instrumented torso phantoms. Source sections reviewed: Detector configuration; shielding comparisons; Methods and unit definitions. Findings and applicability limits: Radiation exposure, absorbed dose, dose equivalent, shielding, cabin location and dosimetry. Measurements depended on location, shielding and mission conditions. This was an uncrewed mission, not a study measuring clinical symptoms in astronauts. Selecting a Moon or Mars environment in software does not supply dosimeter measurements or a trajectory-specific exposure model. Heart rate, SpO2, sleep and reported symptoms cannot calculate a personal radiation dose from this study. Physical exposure measurements must remain separate from biological risk estimates and individual diagnoses. Findings should not be transferred unchanged to different spacecraft, shielding, trajectories or solar conditions.
