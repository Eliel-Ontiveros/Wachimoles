Reviewed evidence summary, not the complete publication. Source: Lumbar Spine Paraspinal Muscle and Intervertebral Disc Height Changes in Astronauts After Long-Duration Spaceflight on the International Space Station. Authors/institution: Chang DG et al.. Year: 2016. Evidence and environment: Human flight, longitudinal MRI; six ISS crewmembers; Earth recovery. Source sections reviewed: Materials and Methods; Results; Discussion. Findings and applicability limits: Lower-back discomfort, lumbar spine, paraspinal muscles, multifidus, disc height. MRI demonstrated reduced functional paraspinal muscle cross-sectional area after long-duration flight, with incomplete recovery at follow-up. The study did not demonstrate generalized intervertebral disc-height expansion. These are small-cohort imaging observations, not evidence identifying the cause of a reported episode of back pain. Atrophy does not establish an acute muscle injury, herniation, or the cause of swelling. Results concern ISS exposure and terrestrial recovery, not observations during lunar or Martian residence.
