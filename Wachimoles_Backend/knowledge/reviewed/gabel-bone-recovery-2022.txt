Reviewed evidence summary, not the complete publication. Source: Incomplete recovery of bone strength and trabecular microarchitecture at the distal tibia 1 year after return from long duration spaceflight. Authors/institution: Gabel L et al.. Year: 2022. Evidence and environment: Human longitudinal cohort; 17 astronauts; preflight and Earth recovery at six/twelve months. Source sections reviewed: Results; Tables 1–2; Methods. Findings and applicability limits: Bone loss, distal tibia, radius, trabecular microarchitecture, estimated bone strength, readaptation. Imaging showed incomplete recovery of some tibial outcomes one year after long-duration flight. Skeletal sites and participants differed; findings at the radius cannot be substituted for those at the tibia. Failure load is an imaging-based strength estimate, not a count of actual fractures. These population findings cannot calculate an individual fracture probability from heart rate, steps, pain or a wearable. The study does not establish the cause of lower-limb pain or authorize activity clearance.
