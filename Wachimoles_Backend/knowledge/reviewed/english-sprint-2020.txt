Reviewed evidence summary, not the complete publication. Source: High intensity training during spaceflight: results from the NASA Sprint Study. Authors/institution: English KL et al.. Year: 2020. Evidence and environment: Human ISS exercise comparison; 26 astronauts; nine Sprint and 17 controls. Source sections reviewed: Methods: research design and subjects; Results; Tables 1–5. Findings and applicability limits: Muscle strength, endurance, trunk and lower-limb function, exercise, aerobic deconditioning. Astronauts self-selected into exercise groups. Physiological outcomes were broadly comparable between the programs, while some postflight decrements remained. Exercise participation therefore does not establish complete preservation of every measured outcome. Strength, endurance, bone density and task performance are different measurements. Changing standard care and nonrandom allocation limit causal interpretation. This study does not diagnose exercise-associated pain or swelling, prescribe a personal training program, or establish readiness for an extravehicular activity.
