Reviewed evidence summary, not the complete publication. Source: Increased core body temperature in astronauts during long-duration space missions. Authors/institution: Stahn AC et al.. Year: 2017. Evidence and environment: Repeated measurements in eleven ISS astronauts; terrestrial comparison. Source sections reviewed: Methods: temperature sensor; resting/exercise Results; limitations. Findings and applicability limits: Temperature, core body temperature, heat-flux sensor, thermoregulation and exercise. Research using a forehead heat-flux approach observed different resting and exercise temperature responses during long-duration flight compared with terrestrial measurements. Measurement site, method, timing and activity state matter. A manually entered spot temperature or skin temperature is not automatically comparable to the study’s core-temperature estimate. The small cohort and particular sensor limit generalization. Associations with inflammatory markers do not prove a causal mechanism. These observations cannot diagnose infection or fever from an isolated user entry or define a personal safe exercise limit.
