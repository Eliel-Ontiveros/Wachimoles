Reviewed evidence summary, not the complete publication. Source: The Effects of Long Duration Spaceflight on Sensorimotor Control and Cognition. Authors/institution: Tays GD et al.. Year: 2021. Evidence and environment: Human longitudinal study; fifteen astronauts; six-month ISS missions and Earth recovery. Source sections reviewed: Functional Mobility/SOT methods; Results; recovery analyses. Findings and applicability limits: Balance, mobility, bimanual coordination, sensory reweighting and functional limitation. Sensorimotor task performance changed around long-duration flight, while selected cognitive measures were maintained. Different tests measure different functions; recovery should not be reduced to a universal date for every astronaut or outcome. Small samples, task selection and practice effects limit inference. Reported movement difficulty cannot be attributed to muscle strength alone, and a pain rating is not a substitute for task testing. This study does not diagnose vestibular disease or clear a person for exercise, walking, landing tasks or EVA.
