Reviewed evidence summary, not the complete publication. Source: Circadian misalignment affects sleep and medication use before and during spaceflight. Authors/institution: Flynn-Evans EE et al.. Year: 2016. Evidence and environment: Observational human ISS study; 21 participants; actigraphy/light data and modeled circadian phase. Source sections reviewed: Results; circadian alignment classification; limitations. Findings and applicability limits: Sleep timing, circadian misalignment, sleep duration and fatigue. Modeled misalignment was associated with shorter sleep episodes. Observational associations do not establish the cause of one person’s fatigue. Sleep duration alone does not reveal circadian phase, sleep architecture, fragmentation or subjective sleep quality. The analysis used research data beyond a single total-sleep-duration value. Some ISS sleep data overlap with the Barger observational work, so these documents are not independent replication. This summary does not provide hypnotic recommendations, diagnose insomnia, or establish individual performance readiness.
